# Decision Journal Prompts — Capturing What Only the Clerk Knows

Statute binds every Michigan clerk identically; the discretion layer — how THIS
office actually runs — lives in the clerk's head and is what a substitute needs
most. Answer 2-3 prompts a week with `templates/decision-journal-template.md`;
save to `corpus/` with `AUTHORITY: OFFICE-PRACTICE` as the first line.

## FOIA practice
1. **Fee practice:** when do you actually charge, what do you waive, how do you
   compute labor — and the requests where fees became a fight.
2. **The attorney line:** which request types ALWAYS go to counsel, which never
   do, and the judgment zone in between — with the examples that defined it.
3. **Redaction habits:** your conventions (tools, logging, double-check) and the
   exemptions this office invokes most.
4. **Difficult requesters:** your practices for serial/vexatious requesters,
   expedited pleas, and requests aimed at other agencies' records.
5. **Extensions:** when you invoke the statutory extension vs. push to meet the
   original date.

## Elections
6. **The cycle rhythm:** for each election type this city runs, the sequence of
   work backwards from election day as YOU run it — not the statute's version,
   your checklist reality.
7. **Precinct logistics:** locations with quirks, equipment history, supplier
   contacts, delivery timing.
8. **Election workers:** recruitment reality, who's reliable, training approach,
   party-balance management.
9. **AV/absentee handling:** this office's process from application through
   tabulation handoff — where errors have happened or nearly happened.
10. **The county/SOS relationships:** who you actually call at the county clerk's
    office and Bureau of Elections, and for what.
11. **Near-misses:** every election near-miss you remember — what almost went
    wrong and the check that now prevents it.

## Meetings, minutes, notices
12. **Notice practice:** where notices are posted (all locations), lead-time
    habits beyond statutory minimums, who verifies posting.
13. **Minutes style:** this council's expectations — level of detail, how motions
    are captured, draft-approval flow, correction handling.
14. **Closed sessions:** how this body handles closed-session minutes custody and
    the grounds it has actually used.
15. **The council's quirks:** unwritten expectations of the body a substitute
    would trip over.

## Records & licensing
16. **Retention practice:** how disposal actually happens here (schedule ref,
    approvals, documentation), and what's stored where physically.
17. **Vital records** (if this office handles them): verification practices and
    the requests you refuse.
18. **Licensing calendar:** every license/permit type this office issues, its
    renewal rhythm, and the enforcement handoffs.
19. **The filing system decoder:** the conventions someone needs to find anything
    in this office — physical and digital.

## Administration & philosophy
20. **Boards & commissions:** appointment tracking, term math, oath practice,
    the bodies with special rules.
21. **Council meeting-day runbook:** your actual meeting-day sequence, from
    packet assembly to post-meeting filings.
22. **Delegation map:** what staff decide without you; what always crosses the
    clerk's desk; who is strong at what.
23. **The calendar you keep in your head:** recurring statutory dates by month —
    the annual rhythm of the office.
24. **Transparency posture:** where you go beyond the statutory minimum on
    openness, and why.
25. **The three decisions you'd most want to explain to your successor** — the
    ones where, reversed without context, real damage follows.

*Completion check: a substitute can answer "how does THIS office handle X — and is
that law or choice?" for every category, from the corpus alone.*
