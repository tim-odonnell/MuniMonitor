# Substitute Drill — Continuity Readiness for the City Clerk's Office

**The question:** *If the clerk is suddenly out — especially near an election —
could the assistant guide the deputy or a temporary appointee well enough, and how
do we find out before it matters?*

Third sibling of the assessor and building-department drills; same method.

## Two hard truths

**1. The legal constraint.** The city clerk is a statutory officer with duties an
AI cannot hold: election administration (with state accreditation requirements for
election officials), certifications, oaths, official signatures, custody of
records. A substitute must be a lawfully-acting deputy or appointee; the assistant
is the best onboarding tool that person ever had — never the officeholder.

**2. The substitute paradox, sharpened by the calendar.** A clerk's absence isn't
dangerous on an average Tuesday — it's dangerous 30 days before an election or
mid-FOIA-clock. The person relying on the assistant is least able to catch its
errors precisely when deadlines are shortest and mistakes are irreversible
(a missed election notice cannot be re-run; an unlawful disclosure cannot be
recalled; an OMA notice failure can void council action).

## The zero-tolerance classes for this office

1. **Election-critical silent failure** — a confidently wrong answer about an
   election deadline, notice, or ballot process = automatic drill FAIL.
2. **Disclosure silent failure** — guidance that would release exempt/confidential
   material (FOIA exemptions, voter data, vital records) without flagging review
   = automatic drill FAIL.

## The gap list

1. No access to the FOIA log / request tracking — "what's on the clock right now?"
   is unanswerable without the state-of-office doc.
2. No election-calendar awareness — the corpus holds election LAW; the cycle's
   actual dates live in the SOS calendar, which goes stale every cycle (this
   office's equivalent of the assessor's annual IRM).
3. Judgment history uncaptured — fee practices, redaction habits, which requests
   historically went to the attorney, board/commission quirks (see
   `journal-prompts.md`).
4. Meeting-minutes workflow (audio → draft minutes) not built — a future surface,
   out of scope until the text assistant is validated.

## What "ready" looks like

A competent deputy runs the office's routine operations for two weeks — including
one FOIA cycle and the run-up procedures of a (simulated) election — using the
assistant as primary guide, with zero silent errors in either zero-tolerance class.
