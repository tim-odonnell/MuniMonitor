# Decision Journal Prompts — Capturing What Only the Building Official Knows

The code binds every Michigan jurisdiction the same way; inside it sits a wide band
of discretion where two fully-compliant departments run visibly differently. That
layer lives in the department head's memory, is invisible to statute-and-rule RAG,
and is exactly what a substitute needs most. Answer 2-3 prompts a week using
`templates/decision-journal-template.md`; save entries to `corpus/` with
`AUTHORITY: OFFICE-PRACTICE` as the first line. Roughly a quarter of coffee breaks
total.

## Permits & plan review

1. **Permit gray zones:** Your standing answers on the perennials — sheds under X
   square feet, decks by height, water heater swaps, fences, roofing overlays,
   window replacement. Which need permits here, and what's the reasoning you give
   at the counter?
2. **Over-the-counter vs full plan review:** what qualifies for same-day issuance,
   and what triggers full review? When do you require sealed drawings or an
   engineer's letter?
3. **Alternative materials & methods:** every AMM approval you've granted — the
   product/method, what evidence you required, and why. (These are precedents a
   substitute would otherwise re-litigate or contradict.)
4. **Owner-builder permits:** how you handle homeowner permit applications and
   affidavits — what extra guidance or scrutiny they get.
5. **Expired and abandoned permits:** your actual practice on extensions,
   re-activation, and expired-permit-with-new-owner situations.
6. **After-the-fact permits:** work discovered without a permit — your fee
   practice, inspection sequence (what gets opened up?), and when it escalates to
   enforcement instead.

## Inspections

7. **Per-inspection focus:** for each inspection type, what do you check FIRST and
   what fails most often in this jurisdiction's housing stock? (This directly
   improves the vision checklists too.)
8. **Partial approvals:** when do you approve-with-corrections vs fail outright?
   What may proceed while a correction is open, and what stops work?
9. **Re-inspection fees:** when actually charged vs waived, and why.
10. **Photo documentation:** your standards for photographing deficiencies —
    what's expected in the file for a defensible correction notice.
11. **Occupancy calls:** conditions under which you issue a temporary CO, and the
    hard lines that must be complete before ANY occupancy.

## Enforcement posture

12. **Stop-work threshold:** what actually triggers a stop-work order here vs a
    correction notice vs a conversation? Give the examples that define the line.
13. **The escalation ladder:** verbal → written → stop-work → legal. How fast do
    you climb it, and for what?
14. **Dangerous buildings:** how this jurisdiction runs its dangerous-building
    process — who's involved, what the first 48 hours look like.
15. **Problem contractors:** how you handle repeat offenders — documentation
    habits, when LARA complaints get filed, what a substitute should watch for
    (names go in the state-of-office doc, not here).

## Interpretations & precedents

16. **Standing interpretations:** every ambiguous code provision this office has a
    settled position on — the provision, the position, the reasoning. One entry
    each. (The highest-value prompts in this file.)
17. **Construction Board of Appeals history:** appeals heard, outcomes, and what
    they changed about office practice.
18. **The injunction posture:** how the office is handling the 2015-vs-2021
    edition question in practice while the litigation runs — what you tell
    designers who ask which edition to design to.

## Coordination

19. **Approval sequencing:** what must precede a building permit here — zoning,
    well/septic (county health), soil erosion, driveway/right-of-way, fire
    marshal review thresholds.
20. **Trade permit coordination:** how building/electrical/mechanical/plumbing
    permits and inspectors coordinate in this office; who resolves conflicts
    between trades on site.
21. **The relationships:** the working arrangements with the fire department,
    county, utilities, and neighboring jurisdictions that exist by handshake
    rather than document.

## Administration & philosophy

22. **Fee judgment calls:** where the fee schedule requires interpretation
    (valuation-based fees — whose valuation?), and your practice.
23. **Records habits:** what goes in the permit file beyond the minimum, and the
    conventions a substitute would need to read your files.
24. **Education vs citation:** your philosophy on first responses to violations —
    and the situations where education mode is OFF from the first contact.
25. **The three decisions you'd most want to explain to your successor** — the
    ones where, reversed without context, real damage follows.

*Completion check: when a substitute could answer "how does THIS department handle
X, and is that code or choice?" for every category above using only the corpus, the
capture work is done — and the drill's state/edge cards should test exactly that.*
