# Decision Journal Prompts — Capturing What Only This Assessor Knows

State law binds every Michigan assessor the same way; inside those bounds sits a wide
band of discretion where two fully-compliant assessors run visibly different offices.
That discretionary layer lives in your head, is invisible to statute-and-manual RAG,
and is exactly what a substitute needs most. These prompts turn "write things down
sometime" into a working checklist.

**How to use:** answer 2-3 prompts per week using
`templates/decision-journal-template.md` (one file per entry). Save entries in the
corpus with `AUTHORITY: OFFICE-PRACTICE` as the first line. Skip any prompt that
doesn't apply; add entries for anything office-specific these prompts miss. Working
through the list is roughly 10-12 short sessions — a quarter of coffee breaks, not a
project.

---

## Valuation methodology (highest value — start here)

1. **ECF neighborhoods:** How are your neighborhood boundaries drawn, and why there?
   Which boundaries are historical accidents vs. deliberate? Which would you redraw if
   starting fresh, and what stops you?
2. **Land values:** What method does each class/area use (front-foot, square-foot,
   site value)? How were the current land tables derived, and when were they last
   rebuilt from scratch rather than trended?
3. **Sales validity screening:** What makes you exclude a sale from a study? List your
   actual instincts — the estate-sale tell, the family-price tell, the flipped-house
   problem — with an example of each from memory.
4. **Quality/grade classification:** For your housing stock, what separates a C+10
   from a B−10 in practice? Which subdivisions or eras do you have standing grade
   conventions for?
5. **Effective age & obsolescence:** When do you depart from actual age? What
   functional or economic obsolescence adjustments do you routinely recognize, and
   what evidence do you require first?
6. **Commercial approach selection:** When do you reach for the income approach? Where
   do your cap rates come from? How do you position against dark-store-style appeal
   arguments?
7. **The weird parcels:** For each genuinely unusual property in the jurisdiction
   (golf course, marina, mixed-use oddity, contaminated site), one entry: how it's
   valued, why, and its appeal history.

## Investigation & enforcement posture

8. **Uncapping chase:** How hard do you look for unrecorded transfers and
   LLC/beneficial-ownership changes? What sources do you monitor (obituaries, rental
   registrations, utility records)?
9. **PTA late-filing penalty:** Do you assess it, waive it, or something in between?
   What's the actual practice and its rationale?
10. **PRE scrutiny:** What triggers a closer look at a PRE claim? What does your
    audit-response process look like?
11. **Poverty exemptions:** How do you administer the locally-adopted guidelines? What
    documentation do you require, and where do you exercise judgment on asset tests?
12. **Ag/qualified-forest continuation:** How do you verify continued qualifying use?
13. **Inspection strategy:** What's your actual review cadence by neighborhood? When
    resources run short, what gets deferred first, and why that order?

## Board of Review & appeals

14. **BoR posture:** Do you present neutrally or recommend? What goes in your prep
    packets? How do you brief new Board members?
15. **Informal resolution:** What's your practice on pre-BoR informal reviews — who
    gets one, what you'll adjust informally vs. what must go to the Board?
16. **Tribunal strategy:** What's your settle-vs-defend instinct, and what changes it?
    At what point does the city attorney come in? What have you stipulated before and
    why?
17. **Documentation-for-defense habits:** Which parcels do you build a defensive file
    on *in advance*, and what goes in it?

## Gray-area interpretation precedents

18. **Classification calls:** Your standing positions on recurring boundary cases
    (home occupations, hobby farms, short-term rentals, mixed-use splits) — one entry
    per recurring pattern, with the fact that flips the answer.
19. **Use-change judgment:** For family-transfer exception purposes, what do you
    treat as a "change in use" and what evidence settles it?

## Administration & conventions

20. **CAMA/BS&A conventions:** What do your user-defined fields, codes, and note
    abbreviations mean? (A substitute reading your record cards needs the decoder
    ring.)
21. **Sketch & photo conventions:** Anything non-obvious about how improvements are
    sketched, measured, or coded in this office.
22. **Internal calendar:** Your self-imposed deadlines ahead of the statutory ones,
    and which statutory dates have bitten this office before.
23. **Delegation map:** What staff decide without you, what always crosses your desk,
    and who's strong at what.
24. **County relationship:** How you coordinate with county equalization — where you
    follow their studies, where you run independent, and the history behind that.
25. **Communication norms:** Your tone standards for taxpayer letters, what you always
    explain beyond the required minimum, and any standing commitments to
    council/manager about notice or outreach.

## Philosophy (one honest entry each)

26. **Assessment posture:** Where in the lawful band do you aim, and why?
27. **Uniformity vs. chasing accuracy:** When they conflict, which wins in your
    office, and can you give the example that defined that?
28. **Borderline uncapping instinct:** Lean uncap-and-flag or lean exempt-and-move-on?
    Why?
29. **The three decisions you'd most want to explain to your successor** — the ones
    where, if reversed without context, real damage follows.

---

*Completion check: when a substitute could answer "how does THIS office do X, and is
that law or choice?" for every category above using only the corpus, the capture work
is done. That's also exactly what the substitute drill's state/edge scenario cards
should test.*
